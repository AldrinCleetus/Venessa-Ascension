The blindspot

Every resilience tool shows up after the fire.

Observability watches production. Chaos engineering injects faults into staging. IaC scanners read Terraform. All of them need something running. By the time you can test resilience, you have already shipped the risk.

What you have What you can use
Running production Datadog · Honeycomb · New Relic
A deployed staging environment Gremlin · LitmusChaos · AWS FIS · Chaos Mesh
Terraform / IaC files Checkov · tfsec · Infracost
Provisioned cloud resources Cloud Assist · Well-Architected · Azure Advisor
Just an architecture idea Nothing. Until Venessa.

Cloud Assist's own documentation is explicit: compliance checks run on already-deployed resources. It answers "what is misconfigured?" It cannot answer "what happens at 3× load with a cold cache when the database goes down?"

How it works

Describe it. Attack it. Decide.

A language model handles the intake friction. A deterministic engine handles the physics. You get a report you can argue with — not a dashboard you have to trust.

01

Describe the system

Paste a diagram, a paragraph of prose, or your existing YAML. The intake model extracts nodes, replicas, concurrency, timeouts, retries, connection pools and boot dependencies.

Our one honest constraint

A risk map, not a prophecy.

A model is not reality. Venessa will never tell you "you will be fine." It tells you what failure modes it found, what it assumed to find them, and how confident it is. That restraint is the product.

01

Assumptions on the record

Every report lists what the intake inferred. "We assumed your database caps at 25 connections — if that is wrong, these numbers move." A wrong input is visible before it becomes a wrong decision.

02

Coverage over certainty

We do not claim to predict your outage. We surface failure modes you had not considered, ranked by severity and likelihood. The value is the surprise, not the score.

03

Anchored to real incidents

Cascade patterns map to known outages, so the reasoning is traceable to events you can read about — not to a model's opinion of your architecture.

In the engine

The failure modes that actually cost money.

SimPy discrete-event physics, not a checklist. These are the behaviours the engine models request by request, slot by slot.

Retry storm physics

Fixed vs exponential backoff, jitter, and amplification across hops. A retry is not free — it consumes a slot.

Cold-cache stampede

Warm and cold hit rates, warmup windows, and what happens when the cache dies at the worst possible second.

Pool & queue exhaustion

Bounded concurrency, queued work, timeouts that free slots too late, and backpressure that never arrives.

Boot dependency cycles

Restarts blocked by services that are not up yet. The failure that survives every dashboard because nothing is "broken".

Blast radius & recovery

Cascade order, time outside SLO, metastable states, and whether recovery actually happens or only appears to.

Rupee impact per mode

Failed requests priced against order value and traffic — so the fix gets prioritised by loss avoided, not by severity label.

Questions

The ones that matter.

Do I need to deploy anything to use Venessa?

No. Venessa works from an architecture description, a diagram, or YAML. There is no agent to install, no IAM role to grant, and no staging cluster to keep alive. That is the entire point.

Is this just chaos engineering?

No. Chaos engineering injects real faults into a real system, which means the system must already exist. Even "shift-left" chaos means shift left of production, not left of deployment. Venessa simulates the fault before infrastructure exists.

How accurate is the simulation?

Accurate as a model, not as a prophecy. It is deterministic and reproducible given the same inputs and seed, and it captures queueing, retries and cascades that static analysis misses. It does not know your packet loss, your kernel version, or the failure mode nobody has imagined yet. Treat the output as a ranked risk map.

Which clouds do you support?

Any of them. The engine models services, caches, datastores, load balancers and external APIs — not provider-specific resource types. The hosted service runs on Cloud Run in asia-south1 (Mumbai) for Indian data-residency requirements.

Can I bring my own architecture?

Yes. Describe it in plain English, upload YAML or JSON, or start from one of the bundled models (e-commerce checkout, AI inference pipeline, SaaS analytics). You can edit the model and re-simulate without leaving the report.

Where does my architecture data go?

For teams with strict data controls, the simulation engine is self-hostable inside your own VPC. Nothing about the physics requires your architecture to leave your network.
